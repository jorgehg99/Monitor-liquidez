Estas instrucciones forman parte de la tarea programada «Monitor de liquidez» (8:20, 15:20 y 18:20, Madrid) y las mantiene el propietario del repositorio. La tarea programada indica cuándo leerlas. Las secciones 0, 3 y 7b están en la propia tarea programada.

## 1. Read the current state

## 2. File shapes (keep them exactly; add no fields)
- ultimo.json: array of rows {id, name, short, group, order, decimals, unit, country, tenor, measure, core_id, date, value, prev_date, prev_value, basis, source, source_url, rating, note, next_event, comment}. Groups: rate, mm, policy, y10, spread, fx, cmdty, oas, credit, infl, infl_core, reg (GDP, unemployment, new-loan and new-deposit rates of Portugal, France and the Netherlands, and Spain's new-loan rate to companies and new-deposit rates of companies and households), tbill (Spanish Letras del Tesoro 3/6/9/12M and US T-bills 3M/6M/1Y), liq (system liquidity: ECB excess liquidity, deposit-facility recourse, MRO and LTRO outstanding, Euribor 3M − €STR spread, Fed standing repo facility, Treasury General Account, ON RRP, reserves, Fed total assets, US CP spread). Unit "mm EUR"/"mm USD" means miles de millones (thousands of millions); keep every row in its current unit.
- historico.json: array of {id, month "YYYY-MM", date, value}; one row per id and month, from 2023-10.
- eventos.json: array of {date "YYYY-MM-DD", bank, decision (Spanish sentence)}.
- probabilidades.json: array of {bank "BCE"|"Fed"|"BoE", meeting "YYYY-MM-DD", hike, hold, cut (0–1 or null), expected_bp, source, source_url, as_of, note}; exactly the next two meetings per bank.
- noticias.json: array of 10 {rank, sector, headline, summary, implication, action, entities, published, source, url}.
- noticias_meta.json: [{"as_of": "<ISO timestamp of this run>"}].
- notas_bc.json: array of {country "España"|"Portugal"|"Francia"|"Países Bajos", date "YYYY-MM-DD", text_es, source, url}, newest first, at most 6 per country.
- noticias_regionales.json (country news shown in the Europa tab): array of {country "España"|"Portugal"|"Francia"|"Países Bajos", rank (null), category "Banco"|"Regulador"|"Empresa"|"Soberano", sector, headline, summary, implication, action, entities (string), published, source, url}; 5–8 per country.
- seguimiento.json (special follow-up of a stress episode): [{active (true/false), title, country, level "alta"|"media", as_of "YYYY-MM-DDTHH:MM" (Madrid), headline_es (one line), status_es (paragraph), metrics: [ultimo ids], desk_actions: [strings], timeline: [{date "YYYY-MM-DD", time (optional string), event, impact (optional)}], scenarios: [{likelihood, name, what, desk}], banks: [{name, signal "neg"|"vig"|"pos", note}], corporates: [{name, signal, note}], watch_next: [strings], sources: [{title, url}]}].
- analisis.json: [{as_of "YYYY-MM-DDTHH:MM" (Madrid), patterns: [3–5 Spanish strings], ideas: [{kind "Oportunidad"|"Riesgo"|"Vigilar", countries: [..], horizon, confidence "alta"|"media"|"baja", title, thesis, evidence: [2–4 strings with figures and dates], action, invalidation, ids: [ids of ultimo rows]}]}].
- preferencias.json: [{as_of "YYYY-MM-DD", n_votes, summary_es, sectors: [..], countries: [..], entities: [..], topics: [..], requests: [{date "YYYY-MM-DD", text, status_es}]}].
- resumen.json: [{as_of "YYYY-MM-DDTHH:MM" (Madrid time), bullets: [6 Spanish strings]}].
- agenda.json: {as_of "YYYY-MM-DD", events: [{date "YYYY-MM-DD", time "HH:MM" Madrid or null, country, type, event, relevance "alta"|"media"|"baja", source, url}]}, sorted by date and time. type is one of: Banco central, Inflación, Macro, Mercado monetario, Subasta, Resultados, Festivo. country uses the Spanish names already in the file (España, Zona euro, EE. UU., Reino Unido, Portugal, Francia, Países Bajos, Alemania, Italia, México, Brasil, Colombia, Polonia, Japón…).
- watchlist.json: {as_of ISO with offset, items: [{item, note, type "empresa"|"índice"|"tipo"|"divisa"|"materia prima"|"tema", metric {name, value, unit, date, prev_value, prev_date, source, url} or null, rating (string or null), summary_es, next_event (string or null), news: [{date, headline, summary, implication, source, url}] (0–3)}]}.
- empresas.json (radar of the index companies, shown in the Europa tab): [{as_of "YYYY-MM-DDTHH:MM" (Madrid), items: [{country "España"|"Portugal"|"Francia"|"Países Bajos", index "IBEX 35"|"PSI"|"CAC 40"|"AEX"|"Fuera de índice", company, sector, signal "pos"|"vig"|"neg"|null, rating (string or null), next_event (string or null), note (string or null), date ("YYYY-MM-DD" or null), source (string or null), url (string or null)}]}].

- eventos_caja.json (corporate liquidity events): [{as_of, items: [{company, country, direction "+"|"-"|"refi", amount (string or null), event_type (resultados|dividendo|recompra|emisión|préstamo|M&A|venta de activos|capex|vencimiento|impuestos|refinanciación|rating), cash_balance (string or null), event_date ("YYYY-MM-DD" or null), published "YYYY-MM-DD", text (1–2 Spanish sentences with figures), implication (one Spanish sentence for cash management), source, url}]}]; newest first, at most 60 items, drop items published more than 21 days ago.

## 6g. Liquidity intelligence blocks (mmf, riesgos, regulacion, eventos_caja)
Think like the head of a corporate liquidity desk: the question is always "what changed since yesterday, and what does it mean for deposit pricing and for where, in which currency and for how long corporates hold their cash?".
- mmf: money market funds are direct competition for corporate liquidity. Keep the latest published figures with their dates: USD total, government and prime MMF assets and weekly flows (ICI weekly report, Thursdays, https://www.ici.org/research/stats/mmf), 7-day yields (Crane 100 and Crane government/prime institutional averages as republished, https://cranedata.com/), the SEC monthly MMF statistics; EUR MMF assets, flows and yields (ECB monetary developments and investment-fund statistics, Crane MFI International offshore EUR funds, Lipper/LSEG, EFAMA, IMMFA); GBP MMF assets, flows and yields (same sources). Then write atractivo: for EUR, USD and GBP compare the MMF yield with the bank-deposit references in this page (new corporate term-deposit rates of Spain, France, the Netherlands and Portugal, €STR and ECB deposit rate; IORB, ON RRP and SOFR; Bank Rate and SONIA), give the gap in pb and one Spanish sentence on whether corporate cash has a reason to leave deposits for MMFs.
- riesgos: 4–8 live risks that could move corporate liquidity (sovereign stress, rating downgrades, bank problems, widening credit spreads, money-market stress, elections or political uncertainty, regulatory changes, FX dislocations, concentration of maturities, geopolitical shocks). For each: what happened with figures and dates, impacto_caja answering explicitly "¿puede esto hacer que las empresas cambien dónde mantienen su caja, en qué moneda o durante cuánto tiempo?", what to watch next with dates, sources. Use the ultimo data (spreads, OAS, Euribor − €STR, SOFR − IORB, reserves, FX) for the figures. Drop risks that are no longer live.
- regulacion: only items that can change competition for deposits or the value of funding: LCR, NSFR, treatment of corporate and operational deposits, stable vs less-stable funding, EU banking rules (CRR/CRD, CMDI, EDIS), MMF rules, instant payments, digital euro, deposit insurance, stablecoins competing with deposits. Sources: ECB, EBA, European Commission/Council/Parliament, Fed, FDIC, BoE, PRA, BIS. Each item with headline_es and implication_es for corporate deposits.
- eventos_caja: public signals of where corporate liquidity is accumulating or being released: quarterly results with cash and equivalents and FCF, dividends, buybacks, debt issuance, M&A, extraordinary capex, maturities, asset sales, tax payments and refinancings, with special attention to large and cash-rich corporates of the four countries and of the IBEX 35, CAC 40, PSI, AEX and Euro Stoxx 50. Each event with direction, amount, cash balance when published, event date and one-sentence cash-management implication (e.g. temporary surplus to place until a payment date, cash need, refinancing that frees liquidity). Never say whether the bank has a commercial relationship: the user decides that internally.

## 6e. Análisis y oportunidades (think like a senior cash management and liquidity analyst)
After the data, news and radar are updated and saved in /home/claude/site-data/, run `python3 -I build_site.py --signals /home/claude/site-data`. It prints automatic alerts (3-year percentiles, daily jumps, deposit-vs-Euribor gaps), a comparison of Spain, Portugal, France and the Netherlands (sovereign spreads, inflation, new deposit and loan rates, margins) and the EUR/USD placement ladders (€STR/Euribor vs Letras, SOFR/Term SOFR vs T-bills). Use it, historico.json, agenda.json, probabilidades.json, the news and the radar to write analisis.json:
- patterns: 3–5 short observations of what is moving together or diverging across Europe (focus España, Portugal, Francia, Países Bajos), each with figures.
- quality bar: the user forwards Análisis and Europa to his boss, so every pattern and idea must read like an expert liquidity analyst: a precise claim, the figures and dates that prove it, the mechanism (why it matters for deposits, LCR/NSFR value, pricing or corporate cash behaviour), and a concrete action with a date. Prefer fewer, sharper ideas to generic ones; never pad.
